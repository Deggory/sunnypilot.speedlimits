# United Kingdom (GB): speed rules for the comma
Checked: 2026-10-29. Legal basis: Road Traffic Regulation Act 1984 (RTRA) ss. 81-86 and Sch. 6; the 70 mph, 60 mph and 50 mph (Temporary Speed Limit) Order 1977, continued by SI 1978/1548; Motorways Traffic (Speed Limit) Regulations 1974 (SI 1974/502) reg. 3; Traffic Signs Regulations and General Directions 2016 (TSRGD, SI 2016/362), as amended for Wales by SI 2023/783; Restricted Roads (20 mph Speed Limit) (Wales) Order 2022 (SI 2022/800 (W. 177)); per-scheme Variable Speed Limits Regulations under RTRA s. 17 (e.g. SI 2016/988); Northern Ireland: Road Traffic Regulation (NI) Order 1997 (SI 1997/276 (N.I. 2)). Guidance: Highway Code, Traffic Signs Manual (TSM) Chapter 3 (2019), DfT Circular 01/2013 "Setting local speed limits" (rev. March 2024).

Scope: Great Britain (England, Scotland, Wales) and Northern Ireland. The Isle of Man and the Channel Islands are not part of the UK and are not covered.

Every URL below was opened on 2026-10-29. Certainty is given as (high / medium / low) where the line is an interpretation or rests on a secondary source.

**Units.** Every UK speed limit sign is in miles per hour, with no unit on the sign. The only sign in km/h is the tram limit, diagram 976, which is diamond-shaped ([TSM Ch. 3 §8.2 and §8.13](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)) (high). Conversion: 1 mph = 1.609344 km/h, so 20 = 32.19, 30 = 48.28, 40 = 64.37, 50 = 80.47, 60 = 96.56 and 70 = 112.65 km/h.

Other vehicle classes (RTRA s. 86 and Sch. 6) are summarised in the [HC rule 124 table](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/general-rules-techniques-and-advice-for-all-drivers-and-riders-103-to-158). HGVs over 7.5 t: 50/60/60 mph in England and Wales, 40/50/60 in Scotland. Motorhomes over 3.05 t: 50/60/70. Buses: 50/60/70. The national speed limit sign therefore means a different number for each vehicle class. The comma only needs the car row.

"National speed limit" is defined in TSRGD as the 1977 Order (60 single, 70 dual) or reg. 3 of the 1974 Motorway Regulations (70) ([TSRGD Sch. 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/1)) (high).

Scotland: Scotland keeps the 30 mph restricted-road default. Lit roads there mean "carriageway lighting", lamps not more than 185 m apart, on roads "of a classification or type specified" in regulations by the Scottish Ministers ([RTRA s. 82(1)(b)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1984/27/section/82)). The Scottish policy of "20 mph on all appropriate roads by 2025" is carried out by local temporary and permanent orders, signed with 670 signs and repeaters, not by changing s. 81 ([Inverclyde Council report 2025](https://www.inverclyde.gov.uk/meetings/documents/19346/02%20-%20National%2020MPH%20Implementation.pdf), [Moray consultation](https://engage.moray.gov.uk/20mph-speed-limit-consultation)) (medium: council sources, not Transport Scotland).

**Motorway vs. dual carriageway for a camera.** A motorway is signed only by the blue 2901 sign, usually on the entry slip road, and by blue direction signs. An all-purpose dual carriageway has green (primary) or white (non-primary) direction signs and no start sign. Both carry 70 mph for cars, so the difference matters only for road rules, not for the number. The TSM states that "The sign to diagram 2901, indicating the start of motorway regulations, also indicates that the national speed limit applies". No 671 is placed on entry slip roads of NSL motorways ([TSM §8.6](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)) (high). The sign colours above come from common practice: no primary source was opened for them (medium).

**Single vs. dual carriageway for a camera.** The NSL sign means 60 or 70 depending on whether a central reservation exists. Painted hatching is not a "permanent work", and a traffic island is expressly excluded ([TSRGD Sch. 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/1)) (medium: interpretation of the definition; the 1977 Order's own definition was not opened, it is PDF only). Where a road with a 60 limit is partly dual and partly single, terminal signs must stand where the central reservation begins or ends ([TSM §8.5](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)).

## How long a speed sign holds

- **Limits belong to lengths of road, not to junctions.** A non-restricted limit is imposed by an order on "a road" or a specified length (RTRA s. 84(1)). A terminal sign must be placed "as near as practicable to the point that the speed limit begins or ends" ([TSRGD Sch. 10 Part 4 para 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10/part/4)) (high). A 670 sign therefore holds until the next terminal sign (670 with another number, 671, or a zone end), not until the next junction (high).
- **Turning into another road.** That road has its own limit. Where a side road's limit differs from the main road's, "a single terminal sign should normally be provided in each direction on the side road", sited "generally no more than 20 m from the edge of the carriageway" of the main road ([TSM §8.2](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)) (high that this is the guidance; it is a "should", not a legal requirement). At roundabouts and signalled junctions "full signing" is recommended. A roundabout where limits differ takes the limit of the majority of its approaches, or the lower one on a tie ([DfT Circular 01/2013 para 42](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/setting-local-speed-limits/setting-local-speed-limits)).
- **Lit roads need no signs at all for 30 (20 in Wales).** Absent or even misleading signs on a lit restricted road are "relevant only to sentence", not to guilt, and nothing in s. 85 qualifies ss. 81-82 ([Humber v DPP [2008] EWHC 2932 (Admin) paras 12-14](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/admin/2008/2932)) (high). On every other road, nobody can be convicted unless the limit is indicated by the prescribed signs ([RTRA s. 85(4)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1984/27/section/85)). *Coombes v DPP* [2006] EWHC 3263 (Admin) is reported to apply s. 85(4) to signs hidden by foliage (low: the judgment itself was not opened, BAILII returned 403).
- **Repeaters.**
  - 670 must **not** be repeated on a lit road with a 30 mph limit in England, or a lit road with a 20 mph limit in Wales ([TSRGD Sch. 10 Part 4 para 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10/part/4), which applies to item 1, diagram 670, and item 9, the 1065 marking: [Part 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10/part/2)).
  - 671 (NSL) must **not** be repeated on a motorway, or on a non-motorway road without carriageway lighting ([para 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10/part/4), applying to item 2).
  - Hence: on a lit road, any limit other than the default (including NSL) is repeated, because "drivers have only the repeaters to tell them that the limit is not 30 mph" ([TSM §8.3](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)).
  - In Wales after 2023, 30 repeaters are allowed on lit 30 roads, and 20 repeaters on lit roads had to be removed by 17 Sept 2024 (road roundels by 17 Sept 2028) ([Welsh Government TSRGD guidance, Aug 2023, §2 and §4](https://www.gov.wales/sites/default/files/publications/2023-08/traffic-signs-regulations-and-general-directions-guidance-on-the-introduction-of-the-default-20mph-speed-limit.pdf)).
  - Recommended maximum repeater spacing ([TSM table 8-4](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)) runs from about 200 m (20 mph) and 250 m (30 mph unlit) to 600 m (NSL on a lit dual carriageway) (medium: column alignment read from extracted PDF text). "There is no specific requirement to provide repeater signs" (TSM §8.3.1, quoted in the Welsh guidance §4.2).
- **Ramps and slip roads.**
  - Slip roads of a motorway are part of the motorway (they are in the motorway scheme schedules, e.g. [SI 2016/988 Schedule](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/988/made)).
  - Where a motorway on the NSL meets an all-purpose road with a different limit, **670 terminal signs must be on the exit slip road** showing the limit of the all-purpose road ([TSM §8.6](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)) (high). A number read on a GB exit slip is therefore usually the start of the next road's limit, not a ramp-only limit.
  - Link roads and slip roads may have their own limits. "Where the speed limit on a motorway ends and becomes the national limit, terminal signs to diagram 671 must be erected" (TSM §8.6).
  - Where a motorway begins with a limit other than the NSL, a 670 repeater stands next to 2901 so drivers do not assume 70 (TSM §8.6).
  - Legally, a lower limit on a slip road runs to its terminal sign, not to "the merge" (high for the principle; how often a terminal sign is missing in practice: not found).
  - A permanent limit on the main carriageway applies to traffic joining it, because the limit belongs to the road length. Whether the joining driver can be convicted without having passed a sign falls under s. 85(4) (medium).
  - A **variable** limit applies to a vehicle only once it "has passed a speed limit sign" ([SI 2016/988 reg. 3(2)(b)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/988/regulation/3/made)). This is why VSL signs are placed on the entry slips (high for that scheme; other schemes are drafted alike, medium).
- **Interchanges.** A short two-way link between roads with different limits may take the limit of the adjoining road, to avoid a short NSL stretch ([TSM §8.5](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)).
- **"Countdown" signs** in advance of a limit change are not prescribed and must not be used without authorisation. In England there is a policy not to authorise them (TSM §8.2).

## Zones

- **20 mph zone (England).** Entry sign **674** ("Entrance to a zone where a maximum speed limit of 20 mph is in force"): a rectangular panel with a 20 roundel, an optional place name or slogan, and a lower panel. End signs are **675A** (next limit in a roundel) and **675B** (NSL) ([TSRGD Sch. 10 Part 2 items 5-7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10/part/2)). A zone needs traffic calming so that no point is more than 50 m from a feature, and 674 must not serve as a repeater ([Sch. 10 Part 4 para 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10/part/4); [TSM §8.7](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)). 674 stands at every entrance, including side roads.
- **20 mph limit (not a zone).** Ordinary 670 terminal signs plus 670 repeaters, without traffic calming ([DfT Circular 01/2013 para 84](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/setting-local-speed-limits/setting-local-speed-limits)).
- **Wales.** Since 17 Sept 2023 the words "In relation to England" were inserted into items 5-7 by [SI 2023/783](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10/part/2). Zone signs 674/675A/675B no longer apply in Wales and had to be removed by 17 Sept 2024. The 20 default makes zones unnecessary ([Welsh Government guidance §5](https://www.gov.wales/sites/default/files/publications/2023-08/traffic-signs-regulations-and-general-directions-guidance-on-the-introduction-of-the-default-20mph-speed-limit.pdf)). A new informatory sign 883.1, "End of traffic calmed area", may stand where zone exits were. It is **not** a speed sign (same guidance §7.1).
- **Scotland.** Whether 20 mph zone signs remain valid there was not verified. The consolidated text reads "In relation to England", but the words came from a Welsh instrument, which can only amend TSRGD as it applies in Wales (low/medium).
- **40 mph zone.** Sign **676**, "Entrance to a zone where a maximum speed limit of 40 mph is in force". It is meant for areas such as National Parks where repeaters would be intrusive. It must not be varied to another number, and there is no zone-end sign ([TSRGD Sch. 10 Part 2 item 8](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10/part/2); [TSM §8.4](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)).
- GB has no "recommended" area limits of the Swedish kind. Advisory speeds are covered under Special cases.

## Variable message signs

- **Mandatory.** A speed in a **red ring** on a gantry or roadside VMS is diagram 670 displayed as a light-emitting VMS. Up to 1300 mm diameter is allowed ([TSRGD Sch. 10 Part 2 item 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10/part/2)). Drivers "MUST NOT exceed ... a speed limit displayed within a red circle on a sign" ([HC rule 261](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/motorways-253-to-273)). "Any speed limits displayed within a red circle must be complied with" ([National Highways: variable speed limits](https://nationalhighways.co.uk/safety/driving-on-our-roads/variable-speed-limits/)) (high).
- **Legal mechanism (example scheme)** ([SI 2016/988 reg. 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/988/regulation/3/made)), made under RTRA s. 17(2)-(3) (high for this scheme):
  - The variable limit applies once the vehicle has passed a 670 "speed limit sign". It lasts until the vehicle passes **another 670 showing a different speed or a 671 NSL sign**, or leaves the roads in the Schedule.
  - The limit is the speed shown when the vehicle passes, "or, if higher, the speed limit shown by the sign ten seconds before".
  - A sign that showed no limit or the NSL ten seconds before is treated as showing no limit.
  - Other smart-motorway schemes have their own regulations of the same form (medium: only this one was opened).
- **Advisory.** A speed shown **without a red ring** (Sch. 15 diagram **6001**, "Temporary maximum speed advised", usually with flashing amber lamps) is advice. "Speed limits that are not shown in a red circle are the maximum speeds advised for the prevailing conditions" ([TSRGD Sch. 15 Part 2 item 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/15); [Know your traffic signs: motorway signals](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/know-your-traffic-signs/motorway-signs-signals-and-road-markings), updated 5 Dec 2023) (high). After amber signals, HC rule 257 says to "only increase your speed when you pass a signal that is not flashing", or a sign showing the national speed limit or "END" ([HC rule 257](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/motorways-253-to-273)). Sch. 15 diagram **6012** is "End of temporary restrictions" (its appearance was not verified).
- **Blank or dark panels.** **A blank sign is not an end sign.**
  - National Highways: "If a display is blank or unlit it means that the previous instructions must still be complied with. A blank sign does not negate or cancel the lower speed limit displayed on a previous gantry ... Until you pass such a [national speed limit] symbol the lower speed limit will continue to apply" ([National Highways](https://nationalhighways.co.uk/safety/driving-on-our-roads/variable-speed-limits/)) (high).
  - This matches reg. 3(2)(c) of SI 2016/988, which ends a VSL only on a different number or a 671.
  - A driver who has passed no VSL sign is under the normal limit: the NSL, 70 on a motorway (high).
  - **Over the hard shoulder**, a red X or a blank sign means the hard shoulder **must not** be used. It may be used as a lane only when a speed limit is shown above it ([HC rule 269](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/motorways-253-to-273); [TSRGD Sch. 15 Part 5 para 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/15)).
- **Lanes.** Signals mounted overhead "may apply to individual lanes"; those on the verge or central reservation apply to the whole carriageway ([Know your traffic signs](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/know-your-traffic-signs/motorway-signs-signals-and-road-markings); [HC rule 256](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/motorways-253-to-273)). The VSL regulations define the speed limit sign as directed "at traffic on the carriageway on which the vehicle is being driven" ([SI 2016/988 reg. 3(5)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/988/regulation/3/made)). Different legal limits per lane were not found (medium).
- **Red X** (Sch. 15 diagrams 6031.1/6031.2) closes a lane. Traffic may not re-enter it until a 670 or 671 sign, or "END", is shown for that lane ([TSRGD Sch. 15 Part 1 para 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/15)). It is not a speed sign.
- Sch. 15 signals may only be placed on or near motorways and all-purpose dual carriageways ([Sch. 15 Part 5 para 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/15)).
- **Vehicle-activated signs** that light a 670 only when a speeding vehicle is detected may add "SLOW DOWN" ([TSRGD Sch. 10 Part 3 para 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10)). They show the limit already in force.
- **Part-time 20 mph (schools).** A mandatory part-time limit needs an order and is shown by a VMS that displays 670 only while in force ([DfT Circular 01/2013 para 106](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/setting-local-speed-limits/setting-local-speed-limits)). The alternative, diagram 545.1, is an *advisory* part-time sign with flashing school warning lights (para 107). Some Scottish councils make "20 when the lights flash" enforceable by order ([North Ayrshire notice](https://www.tellmescotland.gov.uk/notices/north-ayrshire/traffic/00000134200)) (medium). Diagram 545.1 itself: not found in legislation.gov.uk.

## Supplementary panels

- GB speed roundels carry **no wet, time, season, vehicle-class or distance plates** in the TSRGD Sch. 10 table. The only variants listed are the numerals, the VMS diameter, and the zone-sign panels ([TSRGD Sch. 10 Part 2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10/part/2)) (high). "Plates below signs qualify their message" is the general HC note ([HC traffic signs](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-highway-code/traffic-signs)).
- No "ahead" variant: diagram 818.4 has no prescribed variant for a speed limit ahead, and countdown signs are not prescribed ([TSM §8.2](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)).
- **Minimum speed** 672 (blue disc, white number) and its end 673. When used, they are mounted below the maximum sign ([TSM §8.9](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)).
- **Advisory speed plates** under warning triangles: 513.2 "advised maximum speed at a bend or other hazard", and 513.1A, the overturning-lorry sign with "Max speed" ([TSRGD Sch. 2 Part 3](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/2/part/3)). Temporary advisory speeds at road works: 7294, 7243, 7245; 7009.1 is "20 max" advised on loose chippings ([TSRGD Sch. 13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/13)). "Signs indicating the maximum speed advised must not be used to indicate a mandatory speed limit" ([TSM §8.12](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)).
- **Speed camera signs.**
  - 878 marks a camera area. Its roundel-free version is often co-located with a 670/671 repeater.
  - **880** (England): camera plus a reminder of the **30** limit on a lit road.
  - **880.1**: camera on an unlit road plus an NSL reminder.
  - Wales: **880.2**, camera plus 20, replaced 880 ([TSM §8.10](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf); [Welsh guidance §7.3](https://www.gov.wales/sites/default/files/publications/2023-08/traffic-signs-regulations-and-general-directions-guidance-on-the-introduction-of-the-default-20mph-speed-limit.pdf)).
- **Temporary "new limit" signs.** 7032, "New 30 mph speed limit in force" (England, lit roads, removed within 6 months), and 7032.1, a newly imposed 20 limit (Wales, may add "AHEAD"/"O'CH BLAEN") ([TSRGD Sch. 13 Part 6 items 38/38A](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/13); [TSM §8.8](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf)).
- **Backing boards.** A yellow or grey rectangular backing board is allowed. A 670 on a yellow *circular* board is unlawful (TSM §8.14). A yellow board often marks a temporary or works limit, but this was not verified as a rule.

## Special cases for a camera system

1. **mph everywhere.** The number on a GB/NI roundel is mph, so "50" is 80.47 km/h. A model trained on km/h classes must interpret the class value as mph when the country is GB. Values seen: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70 (high). Ignore the diamond tram sign 976 (km/h). Ireland uses km/h. On cross-border roads the unit changes at the border (see `ie.md`).
2. **No sign does not mean no limit.** On a lit road the limit is 30 (20 in Wales) with **no repeaters at all** ([Humber v DPP](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewhc/admin/2008/2932); TSRGD Sch. 10 Part 4 para 2). An unlit road with no repeaters is NSL (para 3). Street lighting (lamp spacing ≤ 183 m, or 185 m in Scotland and NI) is legally the GB "town" signal. Town nameplates are not.
3. **The NSL sign 671 has no number.** It means 60 or 70 for a car by carriageway type, 70 on a motorway, and other values for other vehicles. On a lit urban dual carriageway with NSL repeaters, NSL still means 70, not "town".
4. **Red ring vs. no red ring on matrix signs.** Red ring: mandatory. White or amber number without a ring (6001): advisory. Blank: keep the last limit.
5. **The blue round sign is a minimum** (672/673), not a maximum.
6. **Rectangular plates with a number are advisory** (513.2, 7294, ...). The exception is a red-ring roundel inside a rectangular sign: zone signs 674/675A/676, camera reminders 880/880.2, and 7032.
7. **2901 motorway start implies 70** (TSM §8.6), unless a 670 repeater stands beside it.
8. **Smart-motorway 10-second rule.** If the gantry changes as the car approaches, the higher of the two values applies ([SI 2016/988 reg. 3(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/988/regulation/3/made)).
9. **Wales differs from England:** a 20 default and no 20 mph zone signs. **Scotland:** 30 default, with many local 20 orders. **NI:** 30 default by 185 m lamps; R-drivers at 45 mph until the transition ends.
10. **No seasonal, wet or night limits** in GB law (HC rule 125 is advice without numbers).

## For the comma

- **IMPLICIT_LIMITS row** (km/h, from mph × 1.609344; mph in the comment):
  ```python
  "GB": {"town": 48.28, "rural": 96.56, "expressway": 112.65, "motorway": 112.65},  # 30 / 60 / 70 / 70 mph (England, Scotland, NI)
  # Wales (ISO 3166-2 GB-WLS): {"town": 32.19, "rural": 96.56, "expressway": 112.65, "motorway": 112.65}  # 20 / 60 / 70 / 70 mph
  ```
  Integer variant if the dict must stay int: `{"town": 48, "rural": 97, "expressway": 113, "motorway": 113}`. Better: keep the row in mph with a `"unit": "mph"` flag, and convert once, so a 70 sign never shows as 112 or 113.
  - Mapping: **town** = restricted road (street lighting), not a town sign. **rural** = single carriageway under NSL. **expressway** = all-purpose dual carriageway under NSL (GB has no start or end sign for it). **motorway** = 2901 to 2931.
  - **Extra condition needed:** a region key for Wales, because the "town" value differs. NI uses the England row.
  - **Extra sign needed:** `other_sign:national_speed_limit` (diagram 671; also 675B, and the NSL symbol on a VMS). Its value is 60 or 70 mph by carriageway (single → rural, dual or motorway → 70), **never `town`**. Today's "end of limit (0) → `_general(road)`" would give 30 mph when the road-type network says `town` on a lit NSL road. That is wrong for GB.
  - **ROAD_SIGNS for GB.** `motorway_start` (2901) → motorway, correct. `motorway_end` (2931) → rural (60) only if the road ahead is a single carriageway, else 70. When unsure, the lower 60 is the safe choice.
  - Disable `expressway_start`/`expressway_end`: no such signs exist.
  - Disable `town_entry`/`town_exit`: the nameplate 2402.1 has no legal meaning. Read the roundel mounted with it instead.
- **Intersection rule: no (partly).** A GB limit belongs to a length of road (RTRA s. 84(1)) and ends only at a terminal sign ([TSRGD Sch. 10 Part 4 para 4](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/362/schedule/10/part/4)). It does not end at a junction. Turning into another road puts the car under that road's limit, signed at the junction mouth when it differs ([TSM §8.2](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/government/uploads/system/uploads/attachment_data/file/782724/traffic-signs-manual-chapter-03.pdf), guidance). On a lit road it may not be signed at all (Humber). The map hand-over after a turn (`MAP_SECONDS`) is the right model for GB "partly". Straight on through a junction, the sign must keep holding.
- **Blank variable sign:** **no change.** Keep the held limit. A VSL ends only at a different red-ring number, an NSL symbol or 671, or the exit from the scheme's roads ([SI 2016/988 reg. 3(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2016/988/regulation/3/made); [National Highways](https://nationalhighways.co.uk/safety/driving-on-our-roads/variable-speed-limits/)). A number without a red ring is advisory: do not set it as the limit.
- **Ramps.**
  - An exit-slip 670 usually starts the limit of the road ahead (TSM §8.6). It should not be dropped when the network later sees an expressway or dual carriageway: it holds to the next terminal sign.
  - An entry-slip limit legally runs to its terminal 671 or 670 (TSM §8.6). The `MERGE_SECONDS` heuristic is a practical fallback, not GB law.
  - A VSL binds a joining car only after it passes a VSL sign (reg. 3(2)(b)).
- **Other rules:**
  - Repeaters are expected every ~200-600 m on lit roads with any limit other than the default, and on unlit roads with limits other than NSL. They never appear for NSL on unlit roads or on motorways, so "no repeater for a long time" is consistent with NSL (unlit) or with the 30/20 default (lit).
  - Ignore 672/673 (minimum), 513.2/7294 (advisory), 6001 (advisory matrix) and 976 (tram).
  - 7006 "End of road works" ends temporary works limits.
  - Roundabouts take the majority approach limit.
